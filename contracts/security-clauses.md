# Contract Security Clauses

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. Not yet reviewed. Starting language for a conversation with your counsel, not something to paste into an agreement. The plain-English version of each clause is here so you know what you are asking for and why; counsel turns it into contract.

**Purpose.** The security asks that belong in every critical and important vendor contract, made before signature, because before the signature you have negotiating power and after it you are writing polite emails.

## Breach Notification

The ask: notify us of any incident affecting our data within a stated clock, 72 hours is a common and defensible number, with a named channel and enough detail to act. Without a contractual clock, "we take security seriously" is the notification plan. The clock should start at their discovery, not at their legal team's comfort.

## Subprocessors

The ask: a current list of subprocessors touching our data, notice before additions or changes, and their obligation to bind subprocessors to equivalent terms. Your vendor's vendor is your risk with an extra hop, and the 2026 DBIR's third-party numbers are mostly stories about exactly this hop.

## Audit Rights

The ask, right-sized: annual delivery of their current SOC 2 or equivalent, answers to a reasonable security questionnaire on request, and for true critical vendors, the right to a deeper review on reasonable notice if there is cause. Full on-site audit rights sound tough and go unused; the deliver-your-attestation clause gets exercised every year by calendar.

## Data Return and Deletion

The ask: at exit, our data returned in a usable format within a stated window, then deleted everywhere including backups on their normal cycle, with written confirmation. The relationship's end is the wrong time to negotiate this, and vendors know it, which is why it goes in at the start.

## Security Standards

The ask: a commitment to maintain a program aligned to a named framework, MFA on access to our data, encryption in transit and at rest, and no degradation of the controls that existed at signature. Vague "industry standard security" clauses protect nobody; naming the floor makes drift visible.

## Insurance

The ask: cyber liability coverage at a level proportionate to the data they hold, with proof on request. Not because insurance fixes a breach, but because it means a real underwriter looked at them recently and priced it, which is a free second opinion, and because their coverage is what funds their obligations to you on a bad day.

## Note on Using These

Pick the clauses the tier justifies: critical vendors get all six, important vendors get notification, data return, and subprocessors, routine vendors get whatever the standard terms already say. Every clause you demand is negotiating capital spent, so spend where the blast radius is. And when a vendor pushes back hard on notification or data return specifically, that resistance is diligence data, arguably better data than the questionnaire, because it is what they are like when it costs them something.
