# Vendor Onboarding Checklist

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. Not yet reviewed.

**Purpose.** Request to approved, end to end. One page, because the checklist that fits on a screen is the one that gets followed for vendor number 147.

## Intake

Every new vendor enters through one front door, including free tools, which are vendors with a different pricing page. Captured at intake: what it does, who owns the relationship (a name), what data it will touch by label, and what it connects to. Ten minutes, and it feeds everything below. The AI-flavored intake questions live in the [AI kit](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/ai-governance-kit) and bolt on when the tool thinks.

## Tiering

Run the [three questions](tiering.md). Write the tier on the intake record with one sentence of why, so renewal-you remembers what signing-you was thinking.

## Assessment

By tier, nothing more: [critical](questionnaires/critical.md), [important](questionnaires/important.md), or [routine](questionnaires/routine.md). Send with a deadline and a named contact, [chase](chasing.md) on schedule, [score](scoring.md) what returns. Evidence beats answers: the SOC 2 read past its logo answers half the questionnaire with an auditor's signature attached.

## Contract

The [security clauses](contracts/security-clauses.md) the tier justifies, negotiated before signature. Minimum for anything holding our data: breach notification with a clock, data return at exit, subprocessor terms. This step happens before the ink, or it happens never.

## Approval

Green proceeds. Yellow proceeds with documented conditions. Red goes to [risk acceptance](risk-acceptance.md) or goes away. The approval, whoever made it, lands in the vendor register with the tier, score, conditions, and renewal date, one row, the [starter template](https://github.com/HarrisonWard/Security-Program-Starter/blob/main/templates/vendor-register.md) is the shape.

## Ongoing

The register drives the calendar: re-assess per tier cadence, [monitor](monitoring.md) the critical few, re-tier on the [triggers](tiering.md), and at every renewal ask the one question that keeps lists honest: do we still use this? A surprising fraction of vendor risk retires itself the day someone finally cancels the thing nobody logged into since March.
