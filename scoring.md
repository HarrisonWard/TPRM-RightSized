# Scoring Model

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. Not yet reviewed.

**Purpose.** Turning answers into a rating without pretending it is precise. A vendor score is a sorting device and a conversation starter. It is not a measurement, and dressing it in decimals does not make it one.

## Scoring Approach

Each answer gets one of three marks. Fine: the answer is what a competent vendor says. Follow up: incomplete, evasive, or interesting, goes on the call list. Problem: the answer describes a real gap. Count the marks, and the vendor lands in green, yellow, or red. That is the whole model, and its simplicity is why it gets used in year two.

## Weighting

Instead of numeric weights, a short list of dealbreaker questions that cap the rating regardless of everything else: no MFA on access to your data, no breach notification commitment, subprocessors they cannot name, data they will not return at exit, and any answer that contradicts their own SOC 2 report. One dealbreaker equals red, whatever the count says, because eight good answers do not average away the vendor who cannot tell you who holds your data.

## What a Score Does and Does Not Mean

Green means proceed and file. Yellow means proceed after the follow-up call resolves the marked items, and most yellows resolve in one call. Red means the decision leaves the security desk and goes to the [risk acceptance process](risk-acceptance.md), because a red vendor the business needs is a business decision wearing a security costume.

What no color means: that the vendor is actually secure. A questionnaire measures what the vendor says, evidence narrows the gap between saying and doing, and nothing closes it. The score exists to rank your attention, not to certify anybody.

## Thresholds for Action

Green: approve, file, re-check per tier cadence. Yellow: conditions documented in the approval, checked at renewal, vendor told plainly what would make them green. Red: no approval without a signed [risk acceptance](risk-acceptance.md), an expiry date, and a named compensating control, and the business owner, not you, holds the pen. The program's credibility lives in that last rule holding under pressure, exactly once, publicly.
