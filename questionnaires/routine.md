# Routine Vendor Attestation

> [!NOTE]
> Draft. Not yet reviewed.

**Purpose.** Five questions and a request for their SOC 2 if they have one. Filed, not analyzed. The right amount of assessment for a vendor with no sensitive data is nearly none, and doing that on purpose, in writing, is what makes the real assessments possible.

## The Five Questions

1. What data of ours, if any, does your product hold or access?
2. Do you enforce MFA for your staff?
3. Have you had a breach affecting customers in the last three years?
4. Will you notify us if you have one that touches us?
5. If you hold any data of ours, what happens to it when we leave?

Yes-or-no with a one-line answer each. If question 1 comes back with anything beyond a billing contact, stop, the vendor is mis-tiered, and the [important questionnaire](important.md) is the actual next step.

## What to File

The answers, whatever attestation or SOC 2 they volunteer, and the date. One folder, one line in the vendor register. Total handling time should be minutes, and the file exists mostly so renewal-time you can prove the program covered everybody, which is the question auditors and insurers actually ask.

## When to Escalate a Routine Vendor

Three triggers, all cheap to spot at renewal: the data answer changed, an integration request arrived, or they showed up in breach news. Any of the three sends them through [tiering](../tiering.md) again. The routine tier is a parking lot, not a life sentence, and the exit ramp matters more than the paperwork.
